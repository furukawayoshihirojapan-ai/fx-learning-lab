Currency Pairs, Pips & Spreads

Read price quotes, count pips and recognize trading costs.

Free lessonBeginner~8 minIllustrative examples

This lesson explains the mechanics. It does not recommend a trade, a broker or a level of risk suitable for you.

1. Base and quote.

EUR/USD at 1.0850 means one euro is worth 1.0850 US dollars. USD/JPY at 150.00 means one US dollar is worth 150 Japanese yen. Rates compare two currencies: a change in the quote is relative, not absolute.

2. Count pips, not decimal places blindly.

For many currency pairs quoted to four decimal places, one pip is 0.0001. For many JPY pairs, one pip is 0.01. Platforms may show an extra fractional-pip digit. Always verify your instrument specifications.

3. What the spread costs.

If EUR/USD shows bid 1.0848 and ask 1.0850, the spread is 2 pips. A trader buying at the ask and immediately selling at an unchanged bid starts 2 pips behind before any other cost. Spreads change with market conditions.

4. Pips are not dollars.

A pip describes a price movement. Its cash value depends on units traded, exchange rates and the account currency. A strategy cannot be judged only by the number of pips it wins.

WORKED EXAMPLETwo clear examples

EUR/USD: 1.0800 → 1.0825 = +25 pips. USD/JPY: 149.80 → 149.45 = −35 pips. Both quotes are invented for teaching.
