Leverage, Margin & Liquidation Risk

Understand exposure, collateral and why leveraged losses grow quickly.

Free lessonBeginner~10 minIllustrative examples

This lesson explains the mechanics. It does not recommend a trade, a broker or a level of risk suitable for you.

1. Leverage changes exposure.

With 20:1 leverage, $500 of required margin can support approximately $10,000 of notional exposure, assuming a simple 5% requirement. That does not turn $500 into a loss limit. The whole $10,000 position responds to exchange-rate changes.

2. Margin is collateral, not a trading fee.

Used margin is capital allocated to support an open position. Equity includes unrealized gains or losses under platform rules. Free margin is often equity less used margin, although brokers may differ. Read the account terms rather than assuming all platforms calculate identically.

3. Small price moves can have large account effects.

Assume an account starts with $1,000 equity and controls $10,000 notional exposure. A 1% adverse move on that exposure corresponds to roughly $100 lost before costs—10% of the original account equity. Real currency conversion and product specifics affect the result.

4. Know the close-out rules.

When account equity declines relative to margin requirements, a provider may restrict trading or close positions. Stops are not guaranteed to fill at the requested price. Some products or jurisdictions permit losses beyond deposits. Never rely on advertised maximum leverage as a risk-management strategy.
