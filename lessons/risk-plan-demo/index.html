Risk Budgets & a Beginner Trading Plan

Create a loss budget, practice in demo and record what you observe.

Free lessonBeginner~9 minIllustrative examples

This lesson explains the mechanics. It does not recommend a trade, a broker or a level of risk suitable for you.

1. Begin with a loss budget.

Before assessing a trade idea, understand the money that could be lost. An illustrative budget is account balance multiplied by a chosen percentage. For a $1,000 hypothetical balance and 1% hypothetical risk, the budget is $10. No percentage is universally safe or appropriate.

2. Separate budget from actual loss.

A loss budget is a planning target, not a guarantee. Stop orders can slip, markets can gap, and fees or currency conversions can matter. Leveraged losses may exceed a planned limit and, for some products, deposits.

3. Write the plan before testing.

Define the instrument, scenario, reason for an entry, intended exit, assumptions about spread, circumstances that invalidate the idea and what evidence would change your view. This creates something that can be reviewed rather than an after-the-fact story.

4. Practice, record, review.

Use a demo platform or paper exercise. Record the time, bid, ask, position assumptions, estimated costs and outcome. Compare what was planned with what actually happened. A handful of examples is not proof that a strategy has an edge.
