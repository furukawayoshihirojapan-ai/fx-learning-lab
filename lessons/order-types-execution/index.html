Order Types, Stops & Execution

Learn market, limit and stop orders, plus the limits of execution control.

Free lessonBeginner~9 minIllustrative examples

This lesson explains the mechanics. It does not recommend a trade, a broker or a level of risk suitable for you.

1. Market order: execution focus.

A market order seeks execution promptly at the available market price. In a moving or thinly traded market, the actual fill may differ from the quote visible when the order was placed. Its speed should not be confused with a guarantee.

2. Limit order: price focus.

A limit order sets the worst acceptable execution price (buy at the limit or lower; sell at the limit or higher), if the order executes. It may remain pending or only partially fill, depending on the venue and terms.

3. Stop order: a trigger, not price insurance.

A stop order activates when specified market conditions are met, often becoming a market-style order. It can be useful for managing a position, but it does not guarantee the requested price. Gaps and slippage matter.

4. Stop-limit: a trade-off.

A stop-limit order activates a limit order after its stop trigger. The limit can prevent an unfavorable execution price, but then the trade may not execute at all. Before placing any type of order, read your broker’s specific instructions and test the workflow in demo mode.

A sell stop is triggered at 1.0900, but a fast move results in an illustrative fill at 1.0895: 0.0005, or 5 pips worse. This is a possible scenario, not a normal or guaranteed outcome.
